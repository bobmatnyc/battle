## Design boundaries

- The player's primary job is choosing battle strategy, assisted by a virtual twin. Agent commanders execute and adapt orders tactically within delegated intent and limits. The player may order unit commanders directly when authority and communication logistics permit. Preserve the assess/choose/delegate/execute/report/revise loop; training, remixing and campaign administration remain supporting systems.
- The virtual twin assists with strategic choices and preparing orders using the player's authorized information. Assistance alone does not grant autonomous command; execution requires explicit in-game delegation. Direct orders and twin-issued orders share delivery constraints, versioning, precedence and logging, with one effective order per scope. The twin has no omniscient view or privileged communications bypass.
- Agent tactical judgment is an authorized delegated-decision use of inference, not limited to equal-score tie-breaks. Deterministic rules govern legality and effects. Specify autonomous adaptation, escalation/reporting and fallback behavior; do not require player approval for every local change or grant agents omniscience/unbounded authority.
- All combat units execute autonomously under missions, standing orders and bounded local judgment, including human-owned units. Direct player orders change intent through valid delivery; they never transfer to manual unit control. Communication loss, silence or inference timeout uses standing behavior/fallback rather than requiring a player action to continue.
- Maps and dispatches are the primary player information channels. Both battlefield orders and incoming reports obey period- and equipment-specific communications. A local event or sent report does not update headquarters automatically; map/status changes require authorized receipt or direct observation, with event/collection time distinct from arrival time.

- The POC now has six aggregate detachments in a playable land scenario, plus tiny duel fixtures for component tests and a scripted-versus-scripted headless run. Preserve the headless engine and granular testing path.
- The eventual product supports single player, multiplayer, two or more sides, multiple units per controller, and hierarchical agent subordinates.
- Units are temporary aggregate formations, not long-lived characters. Commanders may train or remix them; preserve aggregate personnel, equipment, proficiency and condition through explicit lifecycle transitions. No individual-soldier simulation, biographies, personal XP or persistent soldier roster. Unit/controller IDs support active command and audit, not permanent character progression. See `docs/research/12-unit-lifecycle-and-training.md`.
- Unit capabilities and state are explicit data; agents choose bounded orders. Models never invent casualties, equipment, supply, successful actions, or world state.
- Current POC architecture: Python deterministic rules with SQLite authoritative state/event storage, as explicitly requested on 2026-11-05. A graph database remains the earlier long-term direction, not a dependency for this throwaway experiment.
- Inference is limited to delegated decisions and explicitly permitted tie-breakers among engine-validated alternatives. Record the permitted choices, evidence, selected choice, and fallback. Models have no direct graph-write authority; replays reuse decisions without fresh inference. See `docs/research/10-graph-and-inference.md`.
- Prefer simultaneous command windows and reproducible simulation. Do not promise deterministic model responses; archive accepted orders for replay.
- Event and outcome logging is a confirmed requirement from the first headless duel. Record order lifecycles, delegated decisions, adaptations, effects, mission outcomes and final results with time/version/causation references. Preserve authoritative versus observer-visible records, aggregate unit granularity, and deterministic replay. Essential records cannot be sampled away or omitted on failure. See `docs/research/05-state-events-outcomes.md`.
- Minimize dice rolling. Prefer explicit causes, hidden conditions, accumulated effects and persistent event state to independent per-tick/per-shot rolls. Any residual stochastic mechanism needs evidence, a defined exposure/trigger, bounded effects and reproducible recording. Inference is not a substitute for dice. See `docs/research/11-friction-and-uncertainty.md`.
- Terrain, season and initial weather are configured before battle. Weather may vary within location/season-specific, evidence-backed bounds; use coherent persistence and reproducible environmental events, not independent unit/tick rolls. Seasonal effects act through explicit conditions. Setup does not prohibit later rule-driven surface changes or battle damage. No model-generated weather adjudication.
- Treat personnel, ammunition, equipment, time, and ownership as conserved/accounted quantities. Separate morale, suppression, cohesion, fatigue, and command trust.
- God Mode is an explicitly requested local observer exception: all formations and interactions may be inspected in live/replay views. Keep it separate from commander/twin/model observations and disable commands in that UI view. Future multiplayer needs explicit scenario/host permissions and must not treat the loopback debug endpoint as access control.
- Every player/controller is located in an aggregate command unit. New battles attach Union command to U2 and Confederate command to C2; moving or disrupting that formation affects communication. No individual-soldier character system. Voice/courier transport is implemented; other technology requires era/equipment/infrastructure-specific profiles. See `docs/research/21-god-mode-and-command-location.md`.
- Keep observation/contact state separate from ground truth. Player and agent interfaces must respect the same information and command constraints.
- Fog of war is mandatory for normal command: omniscient simulation state is unavailable to players, twins and competing AI controllers except the separate, explicitly enabled God Mode observer. Scouts and spies are player-directed intelligence options, with evidence, delay, uncertainty and resource limits. Use aggregate reconnaissance capabilities and abstract intelligence assets, not individual-soldier gameplay or a political subsystem. Determine report production by supported rules; models may assess reports but cannot invent facts or reveal hidden truth.
- The player receives a command map. Design it around strategy, reports, objectives, twin assistance and commander orders. Positions, conditions and communication status reflect authorized knowledge with timestamps and uncertainty; map interactions cannot bypass delivery or authority rules. Preserve headless-first development; the map consumes simulation contracts rather than owning simulation state.
- Support grids over actual maps and terrain interpretation. Keep source cartography, validated simulation terrain and commander map knowledge distinct. Record physical scale, alignment, source dates/provenance and uncertainty; resolve historical terrain changes explicitly. Display zoom cannot alter physics. Unvalidated image/model interpretations cannot mutate canonical terrain. See the real-map section of `docs/research/03-simulation-methods.md`.
- Design for land and sea through shared contracts with different domain models. D-day requires amphibious coupling and larger command scales.
- WWII is the technology/communications ceiling. Players receive period-appropriate battle-management tools, constrained by scenario date, force, theater, domain and echelon. The proposed outer cutoff is 1945; existence by that date does not imply universal issue. The digital command map and twin are interface/decision support, not modern in-world sensors, tracking or networks. Modern-game research supplies control principles only. See `docs/research/14-period-command-tools.md`.
- Consider civilian presence, safety, displacement, shelter, infrastructure, and needs. No political, ideological, electoral, or diplomacy simulation. Fixed scenario relationships between sides are allowed.
- Mutiny is distinct from panic, retreat, surrender, and ordinary subordinate discretion.
- Battle provisioning and force purchasing come later; initial scenario inventory and in-battle consumption are still necessary.
- Include specialist units such as scouts, spies and quartermasters. Proposed representations are aggregate field teams, attachments or abstract mission assets with shared authority/task/report contracts. Distinguish quartermaster planning from stock, carrying capacity and physical delivery; no passive resource creation or double assignment. Role names and capabilities need era-specific profiles. See `docs/research/13-specialist-units.md`; specialist research does not authorize provisioning or implementation.
- Historical unit types need provenance and uncertainty. Avoid innate nationality/ethnicity bonuses; represent documented training, equipment, doctrine, and conditions.
- Keep the POC deployed on this workstation for now; Evo is a future target. Prepare its portable runtime bundle with `make package-evo`; see `docs/research/31-evo-target-and-typescript.md`. Verify its identity, resources, and model access when implementation begins; do not assume they are available or claim Evo testing without run artifacts.

## Language and multiplayer requirements

- Unit and staff prose must suit the scenario's era and communication medium while remaining readable. The user's “anachronistic … appropriate to the era” wording is interpreted as period-appropriate speech. Voice changes must not invent facts, alter command schemas, or bypass observation/delivery limits. Keep modern UI/help copy clear; preserve saved original messages and deterministic replay.
- i18n is required by default across interface, scenario text, dispatches and generated advice. Keep locale independent from faction, nation and rules. Use stable keys/typed arguments, explicit fallback and locale-aware formatting; plan for plurals, Unicode, right-to-left layout and text expansion. English-only templates are not full localization.
- Internal player messaging is a distinct product requirement, separate from player-to-staff chat and unit orders. Specify direct/team audiences, authority, persistence and receipt timing. In-battle coordination obeys scenario communications; no silent instantaneous team-chat bypass or automatic disclosure of private messages to agents.
- Multiplayer supports cooperative and antagonistic relationships plus scenario-defined terrain-control objectives. Terrain control can combine with either relationship mode. Support fixed alliances, multiple players/sides, explicit command ownership and deterministic victory/contesting/tie rules; no politics subsystem.
- Campaign play includes a “Risk-like” strategic map with armies/aggregate units assigned to multiple fronts, reserves and subordinate commanders. Preserve travel/delivery delays, shared campaign time and force conservation; no duplicated troops or loss resets between engagements. Apply battle results once to regional control and force condition. This is a specification direction, not an implemented campaign or permission to add provisioning.
- These capabilities are currently specification/demo intent, except period dispatch presentation and agent voice instructions. Do not claim that translations, network multiplayer, player inboxes or new selectable scenario modes already work. See `docs/research/17-language-localization-and-multiplayer.md`.
