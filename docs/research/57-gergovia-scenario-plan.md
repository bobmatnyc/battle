## 1. Findings on the sources

**Caesar is the only narrative of the day (S60).** The McDevitte translation (Gutenberg #10657) was read for VII.34 to VII.53. Locators are book VII chapter numbers. Other editions differ in names (McDevitte prints the second centurion "Marcus Petreius"; other editions print Petronius; not checked against the Latin here).

| Point | What S60 says |
|---|---|
| Forces | Caesar leads six legions to Gergovia, Labienus four elsewhere (VII.34). No cohort or man count for the assault is given |
| Camps | A greater camp in the plain and a lesser camp on a hill taken by night raid, with two legions posted there and a double trench twelve feet broad between the camps (VII.36) |
| Gallic posture | Vercingetorix camps on the mountain by the town, states "separately and at small intervals", all hills occupied in view of the Roman camp, cavalry skirmishes with archers mixed in (VII.36) |
| The opening | Caesar sees the hill stripped of men; deserters say the back of it is "almost level, woody and narrow" and Vercingetorix has summoned all to fortify it (VII.44) |
| Plan and orders | Cavalry and mule-drivers in helmets make a show; one legion hidden in the woods; soldiers moved in small bodies from the greater to the lesser camp with insignia covered. He "points out" to the legates what he wants and "particularly advises" them to restrain the men: success "depended on a surprise, and not on a battle". The Aedui go by another ascent on the right (VII.45) |
| Terrain | The town wall is 1,200 paces from the plain and foot of the ascent in a straight line; a six-foot wall of large stones about the middle of the hill; the upper hill full of Gallic camps close together (VII.46) |
| Success and recall | The camps are taken (Teutomarus barely escapes, VII.46). Caesar sounds the retreat; the Tenth Legion with him halts. The other legions, with "a very large valley" between, do not hear the trumpet, but "were however kept back by the tribunes of the soldiers and the lieutenants, according to Caesar's orders"; still, "animated by the prospect of speedy victory", they pursued to the wall and gates (VII.47) |
| At the wall | Matrons throw clothes and silver over the wall and beg quarter; Centurion Lucius Fabius of the Eighth Legion, with three men of his company, scales it (VII.47). Gallic reinforcements from the other part of the town gather beneath the wall, cavalry first (VII.48) |
| Caesar's response | Orders Legate Titus Sextius, left at the lesser camp, to bring his cohorts out to the foot of the hill on the enemy's right; Caesar advances a little with the legion by him (VII.49) |
| The flank | The Aedui appear on the exposed flank; their arms are alike and they are taken for enemies, though right shoulders bare "usually the sign of those reduced to peace" (VII.50). Fabius and his party are surrounded and killed; the Eighth Legion centurion Petreius/Petronius hews at the gates, is overpowered, tells his company to save themselves, and dies (VII.50) |
| Losses and end | 46 centurions lost; the Tenth Legion, on level ground in reserve, checks the pursuit, supported by the Thirteenth's cohorts under Sextius; "little less than seven hundred" soldiers missing; Vercingetorix leads his men back within the fortifications (VII.51). Caesar's rebuke next day: they "had judged for themselves how far they ought to proceed" and could not be held back by tribunes and legates (VII.52) |

**Two causes for the overrun inside one source.** VII.47 says the other legions did not hear the trumpet; the same chapter and VII.52 say tribunes and legates tried to hold them and the men went on out of ardour. Caesar's account therefore supports both a signal failure and a discipline failure. The plan keeps them as separate variants.

**An outcome source independent of Caesar, in a thin sense.**
- S61 (Dio 40.35 to 40.38, Cary, Loeb 1914, Thayer's transcription): Caesar "had a great deal of toil to no purpose in besieging" the town (40.35); after frequent assaults he captured only part of the height and "on the whole he was being repulsed", losing "a number of his soldiers" (40.36.4 to 40.36.5); the Romans "were defeated at Gergovia and entirely withdrew" (40.38.1). Dio writes in the third century and mentions no recall. He is later and probably derivative, so he is a second reading of the outcome, not a second witness. His frame is a repulse after a siege, where Caesar's frame is a recall after an object was gained (VII.47).
- S62 (Plutarch, *Caesar* 25 to 26, Perrin, Loeb 1919): no account of Gergovia. Chapter 26.7 says the Arverni show "a short-sword hanging in a temple" said to be taken from Caesar, after a passage about the Sequani. It does not name Gergovia, so it is not used as Gergovia evidence.

**Place and archaeology (S63, S64).**
- The Musée archéologique de la bataille de Gergovie (S63): main camp on the plain southwest of the town; small camp on the La Roche-Blanche hill; double ditch with palisades between; six legions "24,000 men" and "36,000" with the Aedui (a museum figure with no stated basis); Caesar's losses given as "700 men and 46 centurions"; excavations: Stoffel for Napoleon III 1861–62 found the two camps and traces of the Gallic ramparts; 1995–2010 work "confirmed" the camps and ramparts and dated the ramparts to the conquest period; Jud's excavations since 2013 on the southern gate; the page gives no distance between the camps and does not say the finds confirm VII.46.
- Wikipedia (S64, tertiary): site identified with Merdogne, renamed Gergovie in 1865; force ranges 20,000–45,000 Romans and about 30,000 Gauls with no attribution; says modern historians think Caesar's 700 an understatement. The page states that nearly all information comes from Caesar and that no Gallic account survives.

**What was not found.** Any Gallic account or order; any Roman order text beyond VII.45 and VII.49; a count of cohorts or legions in the assault (Caesar names the Tenth, Eighth and Thirteenth only); the position of the Eighth Legion's groups; a contour map or elevations of the plateau; the 1995–96 excavation report (Deberge and Guichard; found only in search-result summaries and not read); the Latin text; Goldsworthy and other modern accounts; the date of the battle (Caesar gives none; VII.55 shows snow-melt in the Loire soon after). The museum page gives no author or date.

## 2. Scenario frame (proposal)

- **Action.** The upper-slope sector of the 52 BCE assault, from the Gallic midslope wall to the town wall, with the recall and its failure. Fixture `gergovia`: "Men of the Eighth Legion under centurions at the wall". Decision: what a forward detachment does when the recall fails to reach it.
- **Sides.** `roman` (attacker) and `gallic` (defender). Player: `roman`, as Caesar, whose command detachment is the Tenth Legion's reserve at the foot of the slope (VII.47, VII.51). Computer: `gallic`. The forward Roman groups are autonomous, as are all units under AGENTS; the player changes their intent only by delivered signal or order.
- **Map fit problem.** The entry grid is fixed at 24 by 21 hexes of 50 m (about 1.2 km by 1.05 km; `grid.py`, `scenario_entry.validate`). VII.46 gives 1,200 paces from the plain to the town wall. At the conventional 1.48 m a pace (a convention, not from the sources read) that is about 1.8 km, 36 hexes, and does not fit. **Proposed crop:** the sector from the midslope wall to the town wall, "almost in the middle of the hill", about 600 paces or 0.9 km, 18 hexes (inferred from VII.46: the wall's position is "almost in the middle", not measured). The approach from the camps and the great valley between the legions fall off the map; they are represented as terrain at the south edge and as a signal-blocking feature (§6).
- **Start.** About 12:00. Sourced cue: Teutomarus had "gone to rest at noon" when the camps were taken (VII.46). Start with the Gallic camps taken, the Romans just past the stone wall. Authored. **Deadline:** 120 minutes (240 turns), authored; VII.48 says only a "long continuation" of fighting.
- **Scale.** 50 m a hex, 30 s a turn. Unit = a century-sized group (see §6).

### Reconstruction (authored)

- **Roman S1:** forward groups hold the taken camps on the recall; a group whose commander is within hearing of the signal obeys; a group within reach of its tribune's voice obeys.
- **Roman S2 (ardour):** forward groups carry on to the wall after a fleeing enemy until contact (the standing doctrine `exploit`, §7 gap 3).
- **Gallic G1:** the wall garrison holds; the returning force arrives by a scripted order at a fixed turn; the mass pushes down only after the Romans are at the wall.

## 5. Victory condition (proposal)

The case compares which groups receive the recall, how far they advance and whether they withdraw together. It has no natural win; Caesar's frame is a recovered force, Dio's a repulse. No existing kind scores this.

- **Proposed kind `recover-force` (authored).** The attacker wins when at least `min_share` of its forward strength stands within `radius` of the rally hex (the Tenth's line) by the deadline, with no forward formation still engaged above `limit_row` (the stone wall). The defender wins otherwise. Parameters authored; the attacker's win is a "force recovered" reading and is labelled so, not as a historical result.
- **Scored metrics, not pass/fail:** turn each forward group receives the recall; furthest hex reached by each; strength recovered; how many groups withdraw within N turns of each other. Doc 34's comparison asks for exactly these. Losses are recorded against VII.51 (46 centurions, about 700 men) only as an order-of-magnitude check, and Dio and Wikipedia say that figure may understate.

**Observation.** Daylight: the 7-hex default. The wall and rampart obstruct sight through `obstacle_height`.

**Communications.** New profile `roman-republic-signal-v1` (authored). Methods: broadcast trumpet signal, voice, runner. Evidence: VII.47 (trumpet), VII.49 ("sends orders", mode not stated; modelled as a runner), VII.45 (the legates briefed in person). Needs: a broadcast signal that reaches every unit in earshot, with a reach and a terrain block (§9 gap 1), and no acknowledgement. Voice `republic-en-v1`: a plain period register that addresses the player as "Imperator" or "General"; to be reviewed against the Pegasus voice ([53](53-pegasus-engine-and-data.md)), which addresses a Major.

## 9. Open questions for the Architect

1. **Sector size.** One cohort-sized sector (about 6 century groups) or the plan above (4 Eighth Legion groups plus reserves)? Larger sectors raise the unit count and run time (a 360-turn Pegasus run took about 2.5 minutes).
2. **Crop.** Accept the upper-slope crop and the start at the taken camps, with the approach and the valley represented by a signal block rather than map?
3. **Why the overrun happened.** Keep signal failure and ardour as separate variants, with neither as the default truth?
4. **Caesar's intent.** Keep "take the camps by surprise" (his statement) as the Roman mission, and leave "also the town" out?
5. **Player.** Caesar at the Tenth's line (proposed), or a legate with a forward group?
6. **Civilians and the Aedui** left out of the default (women on the wall are non-combatants; the Aedui are allies mistaken for foes). Agree?
7. **Headless only?** Same as Pegasus and Concord, or is a desk map wanted?
8. **Sources.** Is a pull of the Latin text, Goldsworthy and the 1995–96 excavation report wanted before a run?
