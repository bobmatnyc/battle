## 1. Findings on the sources

**New primary-grade find: the division's own 1919 history (S57).** Doc 37 recorded that the order that sent the patrol out was "not in the material read". The *Official History of the 82nd Division* (Bobbs-Merrill, 1919, "written by divisional officers designated by the division commander") describes the patrol in one passage (running heads pp. 59–62) and the day's orders on pp. 56–58. It is an interested, post-war unit history that credits York, and the copy read is a rough OCR text. It is still the earliest institutional account found, and it is independent of Cowan (S40).

| Point | What S57 says (locator = running-head page) |
|---|---|
| Battalion order | 2nd Bn, 328th Inf to pass the 1st Bn on Hill 223 and jump off at 06:00 on 8 Oct, compass bearing "ten degrees north of west", objective the Decauville railroad, 2 km away (p. 58) |
| Formation | E Company right, G Company left, F and H in support at 600 m (p. 58–59) |
| Obstacle | G Company "seriously impeded by heavy machine gun fire from a hill directly southwest across the valley from Hill 223"; that ground lay south of the division's zone, "but it was necessary to reduce this fire" (p. 59–60) |
| Patrol | "A force of four non-commissioned officers and thirteen privates was sent from the left support platoon of G Company to encircle the hill and silence the enemy guns", "under Acting Sergeant Early" (p. 60). Who gave the order is not stated |
| Route | Encircled the hill from the southeast, passed through heavy woods on the east crest, descended to the "wooded ravine on the west side" (p. 60) |
| German group | A battalion "estimated to contain about 250 men", many machine gunners. About 75 stood round the battalion commander "receiving final instructions". Machine gunners and infantry lay in fox holes "fifty yards away on the western slope"; other machine-gun detachments on the north and northeast slopes. Orders from the commander's pocket: counter-attack the left flank of the American attack at 10:30 (p. 60) |
| Surrender and fire | The group round the commander began surrendering, "supposing" the patrol "the leading element of a large American force". The machine gunners "quickly reversed their guns", killing 6 and wounding 3 (p. 60) |
| After | All NCOs killed or seriously wounded except Cpl York; "with Corporal York were seven privates", four covering the prisoners, three firing "a few shots". Germans fired carefully "to avoid injuring their own men". The battalion commander surrendered. York put himself between two German officers at the head of a column, took more prisoners from the north and northeast slopes, and Lt Woods counted "three officers and 129 enlisted men" at the battalion post. Prisoners "part of the 45th Reserve Division". Lt Cox saw about 20 German dead in the forenoon (p. 60–62) |
| Context | The battalion that day: about 350 casualties, 45 killed; 123 machine guns, 4 field pieces captured, about 270 prisoners (p. 59) |
| Comms | The change of attack direction reached the battalion only at 10:30, off a dead runner's body, after the battalion had fought 4.5 hours on the wrong bearing (p. 57–58) |

**Other sources.**
- S40 (Cowan 1922, re-read): the valley between Hill 223 and the three hills opposite is "only from two to three hundred yards wide"; the patrol's NCOs were Early, Cpls Cutting and Savage, and York; the thirteen privates are named across the text (Dymowski, Weiler, Wareing, Wine, Swanson, Muzzi, Beardsley, Donohue, Sacina, Wills, Sok, Johnson, Konotski), 17 in all; the gun nests "had been masked and the muzzles pointed down into the valley" and the gunners were "jerking at their guns, rapidly turning them around"; York's diary: jump-off 06:10, no barrage, "17 of us went around on the left flank", first meeting two stretcher-bearers, then "15 or 20 Germans", the leader "told us not to shoot". The affidavit excerpts (Sacina, Beardsley, Wills) describe guarding prisoners "between me and the Germans who were shooting".
- S58 (Mastriano, HistoryNet 2006) and S59 (sgtyork.org, which repeats it): the patrol was ordered by "Sgt. Harry Parson" to Early; the German side was 1st Bn, 120th Landwehr (Lt Paul Vollmer), 2nd Württemberg Landwehr Division, with elements of the 45th Reserve Division arriving 06:10; "more than 50 machine guns"; Vollmer's counter-attack set for 10:30; the valley "about one mile wide". Read through a summarising fetch; the summary of the casualty paragraph was garbled, so no casualty figure is taken from it. Mastriano argues for a different site from S42 (see S43).
- S41 (Gregory 2023): summary only; not read.

**What was not found.** The regimental or company order to the patrol; Company G's strength and commander (S57 names only 2nd Lt K. P. Stewart of G Company, killed); any German war diary or regimental history for the morning (S58 cites archival records but the article was read only in summary); the full affidavits; the official Medal of Honor citation (cmohs.org returned HTTP 403); a period map of the ground; coordinates for either site; the *Army History* no. 50 field study (dead link, doc 34); Gregory's book, beyond its listing and one review snippet; Mastriano's 2014 biography. No unit strength for the German group is sourced beyond S57's "about 250" estimate for the battalion.

## 2. Scenario frame (proposal)

- **Action.** Acting Sgt Early's patrol from the left support platoon of Company G, 328th Infantry, against the German group on the hill southwest of Hill 223, 8 October 1918, Châtel-Chéhéry (fixture `hill-223`).
- **Start.** The patrol at the east crest, entering the woods. The time is not sourced. Anchors: jump-off 06:00 (S57 p. 58) or 06:10 (S40 diary); German counter-attack set for 10:30 (S57 p. 60); Lt Cox passed "during the forenoon" (S57 p. 62). **Proposed:** start 06:45, deadline 10:30. Both are authored; the deadline is the German plan's own hour, so a patrol that has not broken the group by then faces the counter-attack the captured order describes.
- **Sides.** `american` (attacker, player) and `german` (defender, computer). The player is the Company G commander, who sends the patrol and can afterwards reach it only by runner (see §6). Open question 1.
- **Scale.** 50 m a hex, 30 s a turn (engine constants). The 17-man patrol is one aggregate detachment. The map is authored and covers about 1.2 km by 1.05 km.
- **Echelon.** The decision is the patrol's, nested in the company attack. The company line is a pinned background force; the battalion is not modelled.

### Reconstruction (to be fixed in scripted controls)

- **American S1:** the patrol moves by the east crest to the west ravine, does not fire on a group offering surrender, guards prisoners, then returns by the way it came with the prisoners.
- **German G1:** the command group stands; the gun groups fire down the valley until they observe the patrol in their rear, then reorient (delay a sensitivity) and fire; the command group yields when it believes itself enveloped (§7 gap 3).
- **German G2:** no surrender until the commander is directly threatened (the diary reading, S40).

## 5. Victory condition (proposal)

The fixture asks for the loss of effective gunfire and the return of the patrol with prisoners; doc 37 keeps body and prisoner counts out of the pass criteria. No existing kind in `battle/victory.py` (`hold-objective`, `hold-compound`, `seize-and-hold`) expresses "reach, neutralise, return".

- **Proposed kind `raid-and-return` (authored).** The attacker wins when (a) a patrol formation has stood within `radius` of the objective (the rear of the gun position), (b) no effective German gun group fires on the attacker's line (a count of `ineffective` or captured gun groups), and (c) a patrol formation of at least `min_strength` stands on the return hex holding at least `min_captives`. The defender wins at the deadline otherwise, or when the patrol is ineffective. Parameters are authored; (c) needs the captive account of §7 gap 3.
- **Scored metrics, not pass/fail:** time the gun groups stop firing; patrol strength at the return hex; captives held; whether the patrol returned by the approach route. Prisoner and casualty totals are recorded, never scored against S57 or the diary.

The German total (205) sits under S57's 250-man estimate for the battalion and above the 132 captured at the post. Doc 34 and AGENTS forbid an individual-soldier model, so York is not a unit.

**Command inside the patrol.** `CommandEntry` (poc-13B) with `officers` 4 for `A1` gives a step of 17 // 5 = 3 losses per leader. After the 9 sourced casualties three of four leaders are down, which matches S57's "all of the non-commissioned officers killed or seriously wounded except Corporal York". That is arithmetic on an authored rule, not a calibration.

**Weapons and ranges (all authored; the engine hard-caps fire at 5 hexes, 250 m, and sight at 7).** Under `tactics_v11` ([55](55-tactics-v11-threat-range.md)) mixed ranges are allowed, and every unit's class must be in the table.

| Class | Used by | Range (hexes) | Effect | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rifles and pistols` | A1, A2, A3, D1, D2 | 3 | 100 | Authored. S40 diary names rifle and ".45 Colt automatic"; no range read |
| `machine guns` | D2, D3, D4 | 5 | 150 | Authored. Real machine-gun reach exceeds the map; the cap and the ranges are a compression, so the scenario tests facing, surprise and surrender, not reach |

**Observation.** Daylight in dense woods and thicket: authored `observation_range` 3 (sensitivity 2–4). Sourced cues: the men "could not see" each other (S40 affidavits; S57), a warning cry "not heard" (S40). The valley crossing is open ground, where the 7-hex limit applies through terrain.

**Communications.** No radio at this echelon in the sources read; the only delivery record is the runner in S57 p. 57–58. Proposal: profile `wwi-1918-voice-runner-v1`, a copy of the 1775 voice-and-runner profile (`comms.COLONIAL`: voice reach, runner beyond) with `voice_reach` 1 (authored; thicket and fire) and a new voice `wwi-1918-en-v1`: plain American infantry English, ranks Sergeant and Corporal, no invented message text. Voice reach 1 means the Company G commander cannot reach the patrol once it has left. That is the point of the case: intent is set at departure.

**Fire discipline.** The patrol starts with `fire.default_posture` `hold_fire` (it did not fire on the group offering to yield, S40) and returns fire when struck ([46](46-poc13-river-and-fire-discipline.md)).

**Doc 37's R1 and R2 do not translate to this engine.** R1 (one rifleman's fire) and R2 (patrol-wide credit) differ in who is credited. AGENTS forbids individual-soldier simulation, so an aggregate engine cannot represent "York alone". What the sources do differ on in mechanism is two things the engine can express: who ended the fire (a surrender cascade from the German commander, S57, versus a whistle on a threat, S40 diary) and whether prisoners shielded the patrol (S40 affidavits, supported by S57's "careful shooting"). The variants above are those mechanisms. Credit stays outside the model.
