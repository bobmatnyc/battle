# Will battle scale from a skirmish to a campaign?

**Assessment · 5 November 2026 · local poc-8. Decision: proceed to a company-sized command experiment. Keep D-day as an integration target.** The part that scales is the player's job: give intent, wait for reports, decide when to intervene. The current implementation proves that loop at one small land engagement. Multiplying its counters, model calls and map cells would produce a busier platoon game, not the experience of commanding a larger force.

## What the playable experiment establishes

I ran three deterministic plans from the same Little Round Top starting state, changing only the Union policy. Both sides started with 120 troops; Confederate command used the same scripted policy, reacting to the resulting battlefield. Local autonomy, couriers, combat and victory rules were active in all three runs.

| Union plan | Orders issued | Result and battlefield time | Remaining Union / Confederate troops |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reposition using the existing scripted commander | 5 | Held until 17:00 | 80 / 82 |
| Issue no orders; retain initial missions and local discretion | 0 | Held until 17:00 | 98 / 95 |
| Order each detachment to a different rear hex at the start | 3 | Lost the summit at 16:35 | 120 / 120 |

**Design implication:** distance, ground and force preservation already matter. The scripted defender inflicted more losses, but its orders cost 18 additional Union troops for the same victory condition. A general who only cares about the summit and their own losses has little incentive to intervene in this fixture. Choosing to trust subordinates should sometimes be excellent play. Test whether the *right* choice changes with the briefing: e.g., hold until relief, screen a withdrawal, or keep a road open for a civilian column. Do not manufacture engagement by requiring more clicks. This is one uncalibrated setup and opponent, not a win-rate study or human playtest.

## Where the design transfers, and where it breaks

| Objective | What can carry forward | First failure to address |
|---|---|---|
| General, twin and subordinate commanders | Missions, standing discretion, delivered advice and report age give the player a reason to delegate. | Current orders address units; the scripted/model order contract caps a batch at three. Adding platoons makes the player the dispatcher. Give a subordinate a sector, purpose, reserve, authority limits and reporting triggers. The twin briefs on delivered evidence; it executes only within explicit delegation. Direct orders still need an authorized route. |
| Multiple sides, co-op, opposing and terrain-control games | One battlefield timeline, revisions and atomic effects are useful shared contracts. | Human commands are applied to Union, default AI to Confederate; fire treats every different side as hostile. One application owns the current game and clock. Introduce command ownership, explicit side relationships, scenario objectives and clock authority. Test two allies plus an opponent, concurrent orders, a dropped client and role-checked observation/replay. Staff chat is not the player inbox. |
| Land battles across eras; arbitrary aggregate groups | Split/combine already accounts for people and ammunition. Terrain and separate fatigue, morale and suppression can explain outcomes. | Today's infantry volley calculation does not use the displayed general skill score or weapon-class-specific ballistics; reconnaissance skill does affect identification. An icon is not evidence that a capability works. Define documented capability profiles and domain resolvers, then test matched forces while varying range, training, cover, ammunition and period communications individually. Keep uncertainty explicit. |
| Reconnaissance, logistics and civilians | An observation, its collection time and its arrival at HQ are distinct; finite stock requires physical pickup. | A growing map needs contact association and summaries of stale/conflicting reports, not a global intelligence view. Scouts, spy assets and quartermasters need distinct tasks, delays and opportunity costs. Civilian road use, shelter and safety should affect route and mission choices without a politics system. Exercise collective refusal separately from rout; defer force purchasing. |
| Naval combat and a D-day sector | Time, observations, missions, resources and outcome logs are reusable. | A ship needs motion, arcs, flooding, propulsion and damage control. A transport with embarked infantry cannot also place those troops ashore. Start with a vessel duel, then one craft, one unit and one beach exit: embark, delay/divert, land, disengage or lose the craft. Give each transfer a single owner and conserved manifest. A full landing also needs tides, obstacles, supporting fires and wave timing. |
| Persistent fronts and theater command | Formation lineage and immutable battle history support after-action accounting. | A campaign needs conservation across fronts: reserve, in transit, attached to one engagement, or returned with losses. Apply a battle result exactly once, even after replay/retry. Use operational groups on a regional map; expand contested sectors locally. Never simulate the same force concurrently at both resolutions. |
| Period map, weather, seasons and language | A headless simulation supplies filtered projections to the stylized map; the UI can localize independently of rules. | The 504-cell authored terrain and fixed weather are not a historical map import or environmental model. Generalize scale and terrain provenance per scenario, model seasonal effects through documented conditions, and gate voice/courier/radio by actual equipment. Test dispatch translations against the same typed facts. |

**Command-map consequence:** keep detailed counters for a local engagement. At higher echelons show sector responsibilities, objectives, reserves, last report time and exceptions that require a decision. Drill down to individual counters. For example, “left company cannot hold the exit past 17:00; reserve requested” is actionable; forwarding every scout sighting is not. An aggregate report must be assembled from information available at that headquarters. Zooming out must never reveal more.

## Local evidence about runtime scale

A fresh offline 30-minute battle generated **2,562 events and 63 snapshots; replay reproduced every snapshot**. The SQLite file was 41,938,944 bytes. Snapshot JSON alone occupied 39,624,930 bytes, versus 643,152 bytes for the normalized event payloads. The smallest snapshot was 97,145 bytes; the largest 1,039,620. The store serializes the entire evolving world, including terrain and accumulated history, at every revision. When history grows roughly linearly, the repeated history portion grows roughly quadratically across checkpoints. This is a serialization problem that switching database products alone will not solve.

Of those events, 620 were reports sent, 395 were undeliverable-report events, and 360 were local decisions. These are **audit records**, not counts of player notifications or unique failed couriers. Keep the full audit. Publish fewer, better dispatches and keep an evidence trail from summary to source. For longer runs, separate static scenario content, incremental actions/events and periodic state checkpoints; fault-test atomic commits, reconstruction and old-edition replay before changing storage authority.

I also exercised the current reconnaissance scan in isolation on the fixed map, with equal sides at seeded, distinct positions. Median of seven scans after one warm-up, clearing observation memory each time:

| Units | Sightings generated in one scan | Local median |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 2 | 0.035 ms |
| 24 | 66 | 0.778 ms |
| 96 | 960 | 13.909 ms |

More units here also means greater density. This measures one scan, **not full-tick throughput, network multiplayer, inference latency, or a supported unit limit**. The code scans observer/enemy pairs and checks visibility on plausible contacts; fire also searches opposing units. Index by occupied nearby cells, bound pathfinding to relevant areas, and compare output with the unoptimized implementation. Unit label placement, map redraw and polling need independent browser measurements. Keep the authoritative relationship graph for command, supply and contact provenance; benchmark spatial adjacency and visibility in memory rather than assuming one database traversal per pair will scale.

A synchronous provider decision can hold up a simulation interval. Retain local deterministic behavior; request inference on material changes of intent or genuinely delegated choices, with a deadline, fallback and recorded acceptance at a simulation boundary. Do not schedule a model per unit per interval. Proposed TS/Next.js or Vercel hosting does not settle these issues. Preserve Python as a reference, compare equivalent rule outcomes, then benchmark on Evo before selecting deployment.

## Decision gates (proposed, not completed)

1. **Tactics and attention first:** author two variants with conflicting priorities and matched initial resources. Compare stand-fast, scripted and human/delegated play with the same opposing policy. Record mission completion, ground held over time, losses, ammunition, report age, interventions and reasons. Pass when distinct credible plans trade mission time against force preservation and players can explain a consequence from received dispatches without God Mode. If the same passive policy meets every objective, revise the scenario before adding formations.
2. **One company sector:** use roughly 12–24 platoons total, two subordinate groups and a reserve; compare a player giving group intent with the same player issuing direct orders. Record orders per decision, flags requiring attention, missed critical reports, provider wait/fallback, p95 commit time and storage growth. Pass if group commands can achieve the mission without routine platoon babysitting, disconnected groups continue sensibly, no hidden facts leak, and replay is identical. At 60× the present 30-second step allows 500 ms wall time per step; use that as a candidate budget for the complete scripted path, not a measured guarantee.
3. **Separate coverage from load:** run a three-side authority/communications fixture and a two-vessel duel before combining them with the sector test. Require no friendly targeting absent scenario authority, no out-of-scope orders or unauthorized observer feed, deterministic contest resolution, and distinct naval mobility/mission kills. For historical credibility, compare engagement ranges and travel/report times to identified period sources; do not fit casualty constants to one famous outcome.
4. **One beach lane, then two fronts:** test a delayed landing and an interrupted reinforcement transfer. Require the same personnel/equipment balance after restart, exactly one result application, no duplicated support fire and no accelerated reports across map boundaries. Only then expand to multiple waves/sectors and test the intended deployment workloads on Evo.

**Priority:** fund the first two design experiments before a framework port, graph-product selection or larger map. If human attention fails at company scale, faster simulation will only deliver overload sooner.

## Reproducing and interpreting the evidence

From the repository root, run `make headless-poc` for an isolated SQLite battle and replay, and `python3 apps/poc/scripts/assess_scale.py` for the policy and scan probes. The script uses Python standard library, local scripted commanders and no credentials. Reference run: local Python 3.14.8, poc-8, game `e3dbb7d39edc4392`. File byte count is for the completed single-game DB; payload sizes use SQLite `length(cast(state as blob))` on snapshots and `length(cast(payload as blob))` on events. Timing varies by host. No remote job, live provider, historical calibration or human usability study was run.
